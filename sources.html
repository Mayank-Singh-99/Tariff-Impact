Sources & method

Where every figure comes from

No number on this site was generated, estimated or inferred. Each is reproduced from a published source, listed below with its type and date. Where sources disagree, the disagreement is shown rather than resolved.

Standing caveat

Tariff policy was in active flux throughout the period covered, and several measures were under litigation or appeal. The US administration was expected to appeal the May 2026 federal ruling on the 10% levy, and the final Section 301 product list was itself subject to challenge. Figures are accurate as reported on — and may since have changed.

Corrections

Known gaps in this dataset

Stated plainly, because a page that lists its own gaps is more useful than one that pretends to completeness.

Gap Why it exists Effect on the site
Employment data by sector Tariff exposure is not a published dimension of Indian labour statistics. Job-loss consequences are described qualitatively only, and flagged as the least quantified claim on the site.
Exporter-level incidence No dataset separates absorbed margin from lost volume. The gap between the 55% and 70% exposure estimates, and the 0.3–0.4% versus 0.5–1.0% GDP range, cannot be resolved.
Product-level effective rates Exemptions changed repeatedly and are defined by HS classification. Sector effective rates are indicative, not precise. Treat with care.
Tariff incidence by final destination Customs data records country of origin, not the market where goods are eventually sold. Transshipment and re-export effects are invisible in the figures used here.
Current rate as a single number Several overlapping measures apply at once, each with its own product scope. “10%” is shorthand for the Section 301 economy-wide duty. Actual rates vary by product.
Post-verification developments Appeals, further Section 301 action and the Lindsey Graham Act were all live at the time of writing. The site is accurate to — and not necessarily after it.
Maintenance

Updating this site

All content lives in one file. To bring the site up to date, edit js/data.js and nothing else — the chronology, sector pages, charts, tables, counters and source list all read from that model.

Adding a chronology entry

Append an object to the timeline array with date (ISO), display, title, rate (or null), kind, and the text fields. The chart, timeline and table all pick it up automatically.

Adding a source

Append to the sources array with publisher, title, date, kind and url. The sources page groups them by kind with no extra work.
