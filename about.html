About this project

A static, source-traceable frontend documenting the US tariff measures affecting India, their consequences, and New Delhi’s response. Built to be read without running anything.

What this is

Tariff Impact India is an informational site covering the US tariff measures applied to Indian goods from 2 April 2025 to 28 September 2026. It is organised around the questions a reader usually asks, in order:

  1. What happened? The chronology, which separates rate changes from legal decisions, negotiations and Indian domestic measures.
  2. What did it cost? The impact analysis, separating the small macro effect from the concentrated commercial one, and the sector deep-dive.
  3. Where did it land? The state and cluster pages, which follow the duty down to the districts and firms that closed, and the economy, currency and employment pages, which cover trade flows, the rupee and the labour effect.
  4. What did India do? The four response tracks: legal, negotiating, domestic support and diversification.
  5. What is everyone else doing? The global context pages: rare earth controls, the EU car tariff, the apparel sourcing shift, and the transshipment allegation.
  6. What happens next? The four scenarios, with the triggers that would distinguish them and the 2025–26 trade agreements behind them.

Everything is backed by a figure in the data explorer and a citation on the sources page. Every term of art is defined in the glossary.

Editorial approach

Four rules were applied throughout.

  • No number without a source. Every figure traces to published reporting, an official release, a gazette notification or a named research note. Nothing was estimated, interpolated or inferred.
  • Show the disagreement. Where institutions conflict, the range is presented. The gap between FIEO’s $47.5 billion and CareEdge’s 0.3–0.4% of GDP is explained rather than smoothed over.
  • Separate mechanism from outcome. The headline rate, the tariff incidence and the exporter incidence are three different numbers, and the site tries not to blur them.
  • Date the confounders. The West Asia conflict, Brent above $100, Cape of Good Hope diversions, and the fact that the textile closure series begins in April 2023 are stated wherever they bear on a figure, rather than left for the reader to discover.

How it is built

A static frontend with no build step, no framework, no package manager and no external network dependencies. Open index.html in a browser and it works.

File Role
js/data.js The single source of truth. Chronology, sectors, trade data, estimates, glossary and sources. Edit only this to update the site.
js/charts.js A dependency-free SVG chart library: step-timeline, line, bar, grouped bar, horizontal bar, donut, range, split-bar, scatter and diverging-bar renderers. Chart colours resolve to CSS custom properties, so every chart follows the active theme.
js/main.js Page behaviour: navigation, scroll reveals, animated counters, table-of-contents scrollspy, the light/dark/system theme toggle, glossary search, filters, and the 44 data-to-DOM renderers.
css/styles.css One stylesheet. Custom properties for the palette, component styles, responsive breakpoints down to 620px, and print rules.
*.html Thirteen pages. Content-bearing elements are marked with data-render="name" and filled in by main.js.

Design decisions

The visual language is institutional rather than editorial-branded: a navy and saffron palette drawn from the Indian flag, generous whitespace, and tabular figures throughout so columns of numbers align. Charts are SVG with a viewBox, so they scale to any width without a rasterised fallback. Semantic HTML is used throughout, with ARIA labels on charts and a skip link to main content.

Two accessibility choices are worth flagging. Colour is never the sole carrier of meaning — severity levels on the sector page pair a colour with a text label, and the chronology tags combine colour with words. And all animation respects prefers-reduced-motion, disabling both reveals and counter animation.

A light, dark and system theme is available from the header toggle and remembered between visits. The system option follows the operating system preference live rather than only on load. The dark palette is declared once in CSS and reached through custom properties, so charts, tables and callouts all change together.

Limitations

Stated in full on the known gaps section. In brief: no sector-level employment data exists disaggregated by tariff exposure; exporter-level incidence cannot be separated from lost volume; product-level exemptions changed repeatedly, so sector effective rates are indicative; the textile MSME closure window begins in April 2023 and therefore predates most of the tariff episode; state export figures cover a textile and apparel basket only; and several measures were under appeal at the time of writing.

Two pages carry material that is judgement rather than measurement, and say so on the page. The scenario weights are editorial emphasis, not forecasts, and deliberately do not sum to 100 because the external-shock futures overlap the reset case. The transshipment claim is an unadjudicated US estimate that India has asked to have tested.

The site is accurate to — and not necessarily after it. The tariff situation was live.

The short version

India absorbed a tariff that peaked at 50% without a macro crisis, because its exposure was only about 2% of GDP. Exporters in a handful of sectors absorbed something close to a catastrophe. New Delhi responded with litigation rather than retaliation, a framework deal it refused to sign on unfavourable terms, aggressive domestic export support, and real diversification — and the episode ends with the agreement still unsigned and a 100% tariff ceiling still on the statute book.

What the deeper pages add is scale and proportion. National exports to the US were roughly flat through the whole episode while the bilateral surplus narrowed from the import side. The rupee made record lows for reasons that had almost nothing to do with Washington. The textile damage concentrated in five districts, and the competitive comparison shifted against India because four rival exporters were handed a duty exemption India was not offered. The macro story survived; the cluster story did not comfortably.
