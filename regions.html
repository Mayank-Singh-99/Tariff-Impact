Regional distribution

The tariff is a national number and a local catastrophe

India merchandise exports to the US rose marginally in FY26. Inside that unchanged national total, a specific set of districts absorbed almost the whole shock. This page traces the tariff from the federal level down to the firms that closed.

Read this before the numbers

The MSME closure series runs from 1 April 2023 to 31 March 2026. The reciprocal tariff regime did not begin until 2 April 2025. So these closures reflect two and a half years of cotton prices, input costs, freight and pre-existing competitiveness problems, with the tariffs arriving in the final year. They are a measure of textile fragility, not a tariff death toll. The FTAs and the currency moves on the economy page belong to the same overlapping period, and separating them from the duties is genuinely hard.

The distribution problem

Averages hide the whole story

India textile exports grew 2.1% in FY26. Six states account for almost all of the sector, and within them the movement is not shared evenly. Two states grew, two fell, one held flat and one fell for a second consecutive year. Underneath, closures concentrated where the export base is oldest and most export-dependent.

Two variables at once

Export size against firm failure

Each bubble is a state. Horizontal position is the size of its textile export line, vertical position is how many registered textile units closed, and bubble area is the number of jobs those units carried. The pattern is not a straight line: Gujarat closes the most firms while exporting nearly as much as Tamil Nadu, and Maharashtra exports less than Uttar Pradesh while closing three times as many.

What this shows

  • Gujarat closes the most firms and still grows exports. Aggregate competitiveness and small-firm fragility are separate problems.
  • Tamil Nadu carries the most jobs at risk, 17,869, concentrated in a single knitwear cluster.
  • Maharashtra is the only major state declining twice, in FY25 and FY26 together.
  • Punjab barely registers, at 87 closures, because its winter-wear buyers are less substitutable.

Cluster by cluster

Five places where it actually happened

National averages cannot tell you that Tiruppur nearly stopped ordering in the second half of FY26, that Surat recorded 1,402 closures while its export line grew, or that Ichalkaranji has now declined for two years running. These are the five cases worth understanding.

Self-reported at the point of closure on the Udyam portal. The series is incomplete by construction: an unregistered unit that stops trading is invisible to it.

The mechanism, stated plainly

Tariffs do not arrive as layoffs. They arrive as a buyer who will not place the order until they know the landed price, then a factory running three shifts instead of six, then a supplier who cannot pay a wage rise.

That sequence is why the closure counts understate the employment effect, and why the export value series understate the pain. A cluster can hold its rupee export value by cutting price, and cut price by cutting the wage bill, and neither series will show it.

It is also why the 4.92% fall in Tiruppur knitwear matters more than it looks. A cluster that had grown 20% the previous year absorbed order hesitation, a doubled freight distance and an input cost shock, and finished down under 5%. The resilience was real. So was the damage.

What a state export line does not tell you

Uttar Pradesh grew textile exports 9.5% in FY26 while 440 units closed. The state offset textile softness with electronics assembly, which is a far larger and better-capitalised export base. A state-level number is a portfolio, not a verdict on any one industry.

●

Why Gujarat and Tamil Nadu dominate the closure count

Both have the oldest, most labour-intensive textile bases and the deepest MSME strata. Together they account for 2,711 of 7,894 recorded closures and 26,907 of the 59,374 jobs. This is structural concentration, not a new failure.

✓

Where the response is going instead

Central cluster assistance is concentrated where the closures are: ₹516 crore to Gujarat and ₹451 crore to Maharashtra for integrated textile parks, plus six Textile Export Facilitation Centres including one at Ichalkaranji, aimed squarely at the compliance problem smaller exporters face.

The honest limit of this page

There is no clean district-level dataset for this. The Ministry of Textiles publishes state aggregates, the Udyam portal publishes closure counts on a three-year window that begins before the tariffs, and nothing joins them. What this page does is place the two official series next to each other and let the mismatch speak. A rigorous causal claim about which closures were caused by which policy would need firm-level customs data that is not public.
