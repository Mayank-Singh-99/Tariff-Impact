The macro layer

The tariff was a second-order event. Oil was not.

India merchandise exports to the US rose slightly in FY26 and the trade surplus with Washington fell mostly because imports from the US rose. Meanwhile the rupee hit record lows, the Reserve Bank sold dollars heavily, and the Sensex went sideways. If you want to know what actually moved the Indian economy in 2026, it was not the duty schedule.

Trade flows

The surplus narrowed for the opposite reason to the obvious one

The standard expectation was that a 50% tariff would cut Indian exports to the US and blow out the bilateral surplus, which is precisely the number Washington used to justify the measures. That is not what happened. Exports were roughly flat. Imports from the US rose by $7.27B, and the surplus fell because India bought more from America, not because it sold less.

Why that matters for the negotiation

India's leverage argument is usually that a smaller US market share costs American buyers more than it costs India, because Indian supply chains are hard to replace at short notice. The FY26 numbers both support that and complicate it. The US remains India's largest single destination at roughly a fifth of merchandise exports, so the dependency is real. But India kept shipping into a 50% tariff market while the surplus narrowed from the import side, which suggests the US is a larger source of Indian import demand — energy, aircraft, defence equipment, electronics components — than the export statistics suggest. Washington was measuring a deficit against a number that had already begun moving for reasons unrelated to the tariffs.

Diversification

Where the exports went instead

The US share of Indian exports fell, but not because the US collapsed. The UAE and China absorbed the growth. China is the more interesting of the two, because it simultaneously became the fastest-growing major market for Indian exports and the target of American supply-chain decoupling.

The awkward part

India is the only major economy in this dataset that is simultaneously benefiting from the China-plus-one sourcing shift and being accused of helping Chinese firms evade the tariffs built on top of it.

The US alleges roughly $67bn of goods were transshipped through India, Mexico and Vietnam in 2025, and has named the Pune–Gujarat–Chennai corridor specifically for pumps and compressors. India has asked for more detail and approached the Global Trade Research Initiative to check the claim against its own data. See the global page.

Currency

The rupee at record lows, and the central bank defending it

India imports about 90% of its crude. Brent above $100 is a direct, mechanical downward pressure on the rupee, and 2026 delivered exactly that. Foreign portfolio selling during the West Asia conflict compounded it, and the Reserve Bank spent most of the year selling dollars to hold the line.

Reserve position

The hard trade-off, and why it is not a tariff story

Reuters reported in July 2026 that Reserve Bank officials were split on how aggressively to intervene, with the current leadership favouring conserving reserves over smoothing, and market veterans favouring regular intervention to discourage one-way bets. A central bank defending a currency it does not fully control has to choose between reserves and the exchange rate. This is a decision about oil and capital flows, and it is where the real macro policy attention has been. The tariff schedule has been, by comparison, a settled question.

Equity markets

Stocks recovered while the currency did not

The Sensex and Nifty spent much of 2026 tracking tariff and geopolitics headlines, and were roughly flat to slightly down year to date, even as the rupee made record lows. That divergence is the more unusual fact, and it is the clearest evidence that the Indian corporate sector was not pricing the tariffs as a solvency event.

Employment

What it did to people who make things

Textiles and apparel is the most employment-intensive part of the Indian export base, and it is the sector that absorbed the tariff with the least room to move. The honest answer is that the effect runs through hours, margins and wage revisions before it runs through headcount, and that the official closure data is too coarse to measure it properly.

The national picture

The closure figures run from April 2023, two years before the reciprocal tariffs. Read them as textile-sector fragility, not a tariff death toll. The cluster detail is on the regions page.

Why the cotton duty mattered as much as the US duty

India levied an 11% import duty on cotton, which raised input costs for domestic spinners while competing apparel exporters bought cotton at international prices. Requirement for the year was projected at 337 lakh bales against 292 lakh bales of arrivals, a gap of about 45 lakh bales.

The Tiruppur and Apparel Export Promotion Council lobbies pressed for its removal, and it was temporarily waived from 1 June to 30 October 2026. That is a domestic tax that functioned, for eight months, much like an extra export tax.

Worth noting for the negotiation: India removed its own input duty on cotton to help its exporters. The 11% figure is the one a US negotiator would point to when arguing Indian tariffs are prohibitive.
