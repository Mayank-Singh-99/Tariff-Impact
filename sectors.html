Sector deep-dive

Six sectors, six different outcomes

The same tariff policy produced a five-year low in one sector, a record high in another, and near-paralysis in a third. Exposure, carve-outs, substitutability and speed of redirection — not the headline rate — determined the outcome.

Pattern

What predicts who got hurt

Reading across the six cases, the outcome tracks a small number of structural features rather than the size of the export line.

✓

1. Was it carved out?

The single strongest predictor. Generic pharmaceuticals, smartphones, steel, aluminium and auto parts were exempted at various points. Every sector that obtained an exemption — pharma, electronics — performed well. Gems and jewellery never got one.

◆

2. Could buyers switch?

Differentiation protects you. Frozen shrimp kept growing because US demand for Indian Litopenaeus vannamei and Black Tiger varieties held firm. Cut and polished diamonds had no such defence, and fell 8.5% to the lowest level in over two decades.

●

3. How many alternative markets?

Textiles could redirect: 120+ destinations growing, with the UAE up 22.3% and Egypt up 38.3%. Seafood could redirect to China, which became its largest market by volume. A sector dependent on one buyer has no such option.

●

4. Did rivals get a better deal?

Being tariffed is one thing. Being tariffed while competitors are not is another. The textile tariff-rate quota mechanism is the clearest case: India pays 10% while Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia and Malaysia may enter at lower rates.

●

5. How long was the exposure?

Sectors held back for the full five and a half months at 50% had the deepest damage. Gems and jewellery exports were disrupted for several months after the reciprocal and punitive duties landed, stranding orders already placed.

●

6. Who was the domestic lobby?

Pharma and electronics have strong, organised interests and effective representation in Washington. Gems, textiles and shrimp are diffuse, labour- intensive and less well organised — and the carve-outs track that difference closely.

The uncomfortable conclusion

If this reading is right, then the sectoral pattern of the last eighteen months was not primarily a function of comparative advantage or trade sophistication. It was a function of which Indian industries could secure exemptions in Washington. That is a statement about lobbying power on both sides, and it is testable against the next tariff episode.
